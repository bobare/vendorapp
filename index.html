1. Overview

Vendor Center is a companion app for people who run a shop on Jumia's marketplace. It lets you view and manage your own orders, products, account statements, and sales analytics from your phone, by calling Jumia's Vendor Center API directly using credentials you register with Jumia and enter into the app yourself.

The app does not have its own servers or backend. There is no developer-run database, and the developer does not receive, store, or have visibility into your Jumia data, credentials, or orders. Every API call the app makes goes directly from your device to Jumia's servers.

2. Data the app collects and stores

Everything below is stored only on your device, inside Android's EncryptedSharedPreferences (AES-256, keyed to the Android Keystore). None of it is transmitted to the developer.

3. Data we do not collect

  • No crash reporting or analytics SDK is included in the app (no Firebase Analytics/Crashlytics, no Mixpanel, no Amplitude, etc.)
  • No name, email, phone number, or payment information is collected by the app itself
  • No contacts, photos, microphone, camera, or precise (GPS) location access is requested
  • The developer cannot see your Jumia password — login happens in a Chrome Custom Tab on Jumia's own domain, and the app only ever receives an OAuth2 authorization code, never your password

4. How your data is shared with Jumia's API

Every screen in the app (Orders, Products, Account Statement, Analytics) works by calling Jumia's publicly documented Vendor Center REST API over HTTPS, directly from your device, using your own access token. Jumia's handling of that data is governed by Jumia's own privacy policy and terms, not this one — please refer to Jumia's official privacy policy at jumia.com for how Jumia itself processes vendor and order data on its platform.

5. Advertising (Google AdMob)

The app is monetized with Google AdMob banner, interstitial, and app-open ads. To serve and measure ads, Google's Mobile Ads SDK may collect and process:

  • Your advertising ID (a resettable, non-permanent device identifier — not your Google account or personal identity)
  • Coarse, IP-derived location (e.g. country/city level) for ad relevance and fraud prevention
  • Device and app information (device model, OS version, app version, language, time zone)
  • Ad interaction data (impressions, clicks, viewability) for billing and fraud detection

This data is collected and processed by Google, under Google's Privacy Policy, not by the app's developer. The developer receives only aggregated revenue and performance reports from AdMob — never any data that identifies you individually.

You can control ad personalization for your Google account at adssettings.google.com, and reset or opt out of your device's advertising ID in your Android system settings (Settings → Privacy → Ads or similar, depending on your Android version).

6. Your consent choices (GDPR / UK / CCPA)

If you're located in the European Economic Area, the UK, or Switzerland, the app shows a consent form — built with Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP) — the first time you open it, before any ad request is made. Your choice (personalized ads, non-personalized ads, or no ads where required) is respected for every ad shown afterward.

You can review or change that choice at any time from inside the app: open the navigation menu → Privacy Policy → Ad privacy options.

If you're a California resident, Google's role as an ad-technology provider and your choices around the sale/sharing of information for advertising are described in Google's own privacy policy and Ad Center.

7. Notifications & background order checks

If you grant notification permission, the app periodically checks Jumia's API in the background (using Android's WorkManager) for new orders and shows a local notification when one arrives. This check runs entirely on your device using your existing session token — no separate push-notification server is involved, and no data leaves your device to make this work beyond the same direct calls to Jumia's API described in Section 4.

9. Data retention & deletion

  • Your credentials, tokens, and cached data stay on your device only, for as long as the app is installed.
  • Tapping Log out (Settings) clears your saved session and cached Jumia data from the app immediately.
  • Uninstalling the app removes all locally stored data, including the encrypted preferences store.
  • Revoking the app's access from your Jumia Vendor Center dashboard (Settings → Applications) immediately invalidates its tokens on Jumia's side, independent of anything the app does.
  • The developer holds no copy of your data to delete, because none is ever sent to the developer — there is nothing on a developer-controlled server to request deletion of.

10. Security

  • All network traffic to Jumia's API and to Google's ad services uses HTTPS/TLS.
  • Credentials and tokens are encrypted at rest with AES-256-GCM via Android's EncryptedSharedPreferences/Keystore, not stored in plain text.
  • Login uses the OAuth2 Authorization Code flow inside a Chrome Custom Tab — your Jumia password is typed only into Jumia's own login page, never into the app itself.
  • Cleartext (unencrypted HTTP) network traffic is disabled at the OS level for this app.

11. Who else can see your data

Aside from Jumia (Section 4) and Google/AdMob (Section 5), your data is not sold, rented, or shared with any other third party. The app has no advertising networks, data brokers, or analytics vendors beyond the one disclosed above.

12. Children's privacy

Vendor Center is a business tool for people who operate a Jumia seller account, and Jumia requires vendors to be of legal age to enter into a commercial agreement in their country. The app is not directed at children, and the developer does not knowingly collect data from children.

13. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct, export, or delete your personal data, and to object to or restrict certain processing. Because:

  • your Jumia account data is controlled by Jumia (contact Jumia directly, or use your Vendor Center dashboard), and
  • ad-related data is controlled by Google (use the links in Section 5), and
  • everything else lives only in encrypted storage on your own device (use Log out / uninstall, Section 9),

there is no separate developer-held dataset for the developer to action a rights request against — but you're welcome to reach out (Section 15) with any question about how the app itself handles data, and it will be answered directly.

  • Google AdMob & User Messaging Platform — policies.google.com/privacy (ad serving and consent)
